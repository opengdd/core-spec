9. Acceptance tests

Your spec can demand proof. An acceptance test is a check a finished build has to pass, and you write it: a small block in your build plan, numbered, placed with the phase it belongs to.

05-build-plan.md, under the heading AT-4: Alarm on sight

{
  "type": "scenario",
  "given": "a guard on patrol with the player inside its view cone",
  "when": "the player stays visible for alarm.spot_seconds",
  "then": ["the guard raises the alarm", "every other guard on the floor starts searching"],
  "diagnostics": ["visibility-timeline", "guard-state-after"]
}

The fence carries the word test, and that word marks the block as a check rather than an example. Below it you say the same check in one plain sentence, for readers who never run it.

The fields:

  • type: which of the format's four kinds of check this is.
    • scenario states a situation, an action, and the outcome.
    • property states a rule that has to hold across a whole domain of inputs, checked exhaustively or over a declared set of seeds.
    • exhaustive-search walks a bounded space of states and moves, and a complete walk is what proves something never happens, such as no seed producing an unwinnable level.
    • document-check reads your spec's own files instead of running the game, and holds them to rules you state.
  • given: the situation the check starts in.
  • when: what happens. One string, or a list when the check takes several steps.
  • then: what holds afterwards, here two outcomes at once.
  • diagnostics: what a failing run reports, so a builder sees which part missed.

Those field names are the format's own, a closed set that never grows without a revision of the format. A field of your own is legal beside them when your checks need one.

A claim about look and feel becomes binding the same way: a block covering one carries direction_claims, the exact paths it proves. A measurable claim no test cites fails validation, and the validator catches that for you. You make a thing strict by pointing a check at it. Those claims are the direction chapter's subject.

The block never says how to pass. It names no code, no engine, no method: the outcome is yours, the route to it is the builder's.
