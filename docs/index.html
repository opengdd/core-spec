OpenGDD

An open format for game designs that a machine can build.

The game design document (GDD) is where a game is worked out before it is made: its rules, its world, how it should feel to play. Studios have written them for decades, for human teams to interpret. OpenGDD gives the document the final word: you design the game in it, your agent builds from it, and the build is checked against what you promised.

The shift

Agents can build games now. The demos are astonishing, but look closely and most of them are memories: games everyone already loves, reconstructed from the weights. The skill is real. The designs are borrowed.

Building is becoming abundant. What stays scarce is a design worth building: an original game, thought through and written down so clearly that nothing is lost on the way to the screen.

The document

An OpenGDD specification is technical screenwriting. Prose carries the vision; structure carries the proof. The designer states each rule together with its reason, sets the creative direction, pins the tuned numbers, and writes acceptance tests: the checks a finished game has to pass before it can call itself a build of this design.

The ferry departs the instant the bell falls silent, taking whoever is aboard. The bell rings for bell.ring_seconds and never longer; a passenger left on the pier remembers it. Boarding costs fare.embers, charged only when the ferry actually moves. acceptance test departure-is-exact: the end of the bell, the departure, and the fare must land on the same tick.

Creative direction is written the same way. Anything can be a named object, even a mood: the prose says when it takes hold, the object says what it holds.

Crossing at dusk, the world thins: the water goes still, the far shore loses its edges, and the mood shifts to dusk_crossing_mood until the pier lantern is lit.

The reference points the way; the build interprets it. Two faithful builds of this crossing may look different, but they should feel the same.

The contract

DESIGN  →  SPEC  →  BUILD  ·  any engine, any agent

A spec is a contract between a design and its build. Both sides can be you: you write the design, your agent builds it. And if you allow it, others can build your design too, on engines and in styles you never imagined. The contract holds either way.

Every design draws its own boundary. On one side, what every build must honor: the rules, the numbers, the exact weight of a jump. On the other, what stays open: engine, interpretation, polish, craft. The designer draws that creative boundary, surface by surface.

Status

The format is being forged against real games: written out, handed to independent agents, broken, and revised. When it can stand up to you as well, it will be published here.
